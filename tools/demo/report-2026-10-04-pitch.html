The Tablework Verdict

At $1 a seat a month, this is a good tool, not a business — because the whole US market is $2.6–6.5M and a production pays $20 before it closes. At $249–599 a season to the organisation, it is a business someone else is already running profitably. The next thing to do is five phone calls, not code.

The counterparty, and whether this is even in scope

The founder asked for an honest read and reliable income, not a venture outcome. So the rehearsal was run against a bootstrapper investor — Rob Walling of TinySeed — rather than a seed fund. His tests are public and specific, which is what makes him useful here.

The research leg found the alternative was already gone: Calm Company Fund stopped early-stage investing on 2024-12-30 and returned called capital, and Earnest Capital is that same firm under its old name, not a second option. TinySeed is live — a $16M fourth fund closed 2025-12-10, applications reopening February 2027.

Then the count that matters: 0 of 145 portfolio companies are in theatre or performing arts, about 14 are arts/education/creative tools read generously, and 0 confirmed consumer subscriptions under $20 a month. This deal is out of scope before a slide is shown — and the reason it is out of scope is the same reason the business does not work.

What killed the deck

01

The price cannot pay for a customer

A cast of ten at $1 a seat is $10 a month. A production rehearses six to eight weeks and closes, so it pays roughly twice: LTV $20–30, with rendered voices sold at cost and contributing nothing. A single paid click would have to convert at better than 50% to break even. The investor's own recorded prior, before any pitch: "B2C apps are brutal. They're like eating glass… your churn is so high, so much customer support."

And the market agrees with him. The actor's price ceiling has been $4, once, since 2016 — the same number in r/Theatre in 2016 and in 2026 — every one of the ~25 line-learning apps currently shipping is free, and the category leader by installed base (coldRead, 3,934 ratings, 28× the next) froze development in 2022 while still billing $83.99 a year. When the leader cannot fund itself at $10.99 a month, the constraint is price, not product.

02

There is no moat, and it was asserted as a feature table

Counted rather than claimed. The render cache is global — clips.hash is the primary key with no production column, so a line rendered once is free for everyone after. But the hash includes the voice id, so it only compounds when companies converge on the same casting, and voice casting is the one thing a director will want to own. 258 clips, one play, one production. A funded competitor reproduces the lot in days for a few hundred dollars.

03

The buyer is wrong, and the code already supports the right one

Sold to a cast, this is a consumer subscription in a team's clothes. Sold to the department that programmes four shows a year — budget line, next show, a reason to care who went off book last time — it is B2B at a season price. Same code. Productions already nest under an account; roles, billing and invites exist. The only new screen is a season view, and its data is already in the database.

This was a guess when it was written, and the market research confirmed it four hours later. Two products out of the whole field sell to a production rather than an actor, and one of them — Stage Write, free read-only seats for the cast and $249/year team, $599/year education for whoever holds the budget — is the only commercially healthy product in the entire report. The buyer is proven. The guessed price band was too low.

04

The asset that would have been the moat did not save the company that had it

Scene Partner was the only line-learning app that ever worked from licensed script text, with real MTI and Samuel French e-Script deals — exactly the asset anyone would nominate as this category's defensible position. Its App Store listing now returns resultCount: 0 and its domain is parked on a content farm. No cause was ever made public, which is true of almost every death here: the graveyard is not dead companies, it is abandoned listings still charging.

The arithmetic, shown

The inverse needs no market research, and it is decisive on its own:

At $1/seat/month, cast of 10, ~2 months of paying = $20 per production-run

  $100,000,000 ÷ $20  = 5,000,000 production-runs a year
    $1,000,000 ÷ $20  =    50,000 production-runs a year

At an organisation season price of ~$299

  $100,000,000 ÷ $299 =   334,448 organisations
    $1,000,000 ÷ $299 =     3,344 organisations
      $100,000 ÷ $299 =       334 organisations

Five million productions a year is more than are staged anywhere, by any counting method — the venture test fails before the market is sized. Three thousand institutions is a number a person can picture. Changing the buyer moves the required customer count by 150×, and changes nothing in the code.

The research then sized it directly and agreed: the entire US market at $1 a seat a month is $2.6–6.5M, so $100M of revenue would be 15 to 38 times the whole thing. Two independent routes, computed hours apart, same conclusion.

Still unsourced: how many producing organisations actually hold a budget line. The leg found the buyer exists and pays — it did not count them. Five calls to directors answer that better than any desk estimate, and that is already the ask. Also explicitly not searched: dated theatre-tech shutdown announcements (the search budget ran out and fallbacks served CAPTCHAs) and founder post-mortems. Treat those as "not searched", never as absence of deaths.

What survives

One sentence a competitor could not also write: Tablework is the producing organisation's rehearsal record — one shared reading of the play, and a season's worth of evidence about which actors actually go off book.

That is narrower than the original claim and better than it. The three cast-facing competitors share a script or the director's own recordings; none shares a company's voices, and none shows a director who is off book and where. The perimeter — sound cues, room sync — came from the founder's own show rather than from a customer, and should get nothing.

The counter-argument, kept on the page

The original one — that four products selling to individual actors proves the actor pays — did not survive the research; those products are mostly frozen or dead and the rest are free. A harder one replaced it: Scene Partner held licensed script deals and died anyway, so assets do not save products in this category. And the pivot still discards the only distribution the founder owns — he knows directors and actors, not department heads — and buys a season-long sales cycle against under six months of runway. These are not disposed of. They are why the ask is a test and not a build.

And the channels are closed

r/Theatre moderators removed a product launch post on 2026-05-07; r/acting auto-removes memorisation questions as FAQ duplicates; Hacker News has zero coverage of the category across every query tried. Within what was searched, the invite link and the founder's own network are not the best channels — they are the only ones.

What was built this window

The teardown's finding is a price and a buyer, not a screen, and building before the five calls would be the exact failure mode the persona file warns about. What remains for you

Your judgment · do this first

Read the PIIA clause

Tablework's first commit is 2026-09-16, after you started at Gusto, so it is not a prior invention and Exhibit B does not cover it. A theatre app plainly does not relate to payroll or benefits, which is a far cleaner position than impatient.care's — but the clause, the governing state, and whether anything touched employer time or equipment are yours to confirm before any of this becomes commercial. This is not legal advice.

Your network · five calls

Ask five directors what the department actually buys

Licensing, scripts, software, anything — and who signs. Five answers kill or confirm the institutional buyer outright, and no desk research substitutes. You have the network; this is the one thing only you can do.
