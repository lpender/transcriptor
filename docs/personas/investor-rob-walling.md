# Investor persona — Rob Walling (TinySeed)

Rob Walling is a real person and TinySeed is a real, currently-operating fund.
Every fact below carries a primary source URL and the date it was read
(2026-10-04). Any dialogue written later in a workshop is the loop's words put
in his mouth, not his — only the blockquotes here are things he actually said
or wrote.

## Part A — why him, and who was rejected

**Picked: Rob Walling / TinySeed.** TinySeed is the only one of the three whose
published thesis — capital-efficient software aimed at $5–10M ARR, founder
control, no unicorn-or-bust — matches Tablework's actual shape, and it is
demonstrably still raising and deploying (Fund 4 closed 2025-12-10; Spring 2026
batch announced; applications reopen February 2027). It is also the counterparty
most likely to kill the deal on specifics rather than vibes, which is the point
of a rehearsal: its application denies pre-revenue companies outright and its
focus is B2B SaaS, while Tablework is a $1/seat/month subscription — so the
pitch must survive objections he has already published.

**Rejected — Calm Company Fund (the Tringas lineage).** Dead for this purpose.
Tringas announced on 2024-12-30 that the fund "will not be returning to early
stage investing," citing SureSwift litigation and lack of institutional LP
appetite, and that Fund IV's called capital was being returned. Earnest Capital
is the *same* firm under its pre-2021 name, not a second live option — a
widely-made error. Pitching either in 2026 is pitching a wind-down.
**Rejected — Hustle Fund.** Active and genuinely pre-seed ("We invest in
pre-seed rounds. Not seed."), but a $150K first check expecting venture
outcomes, "software generalists," and its FAQ explicitly declines "art/film
projects." Wrong return shape for a single-digit-millions ceiling.
**Rejected — Reach Capital.** Nearest thing to an arts/education thesis, but it
is "learning, health, and work" with AI as the through-line, checks $100K–$12M,
and no arts or creative-tools mandate stated — adjacent, not inside.

## Part B — the persona

### 1. Career and firm facts (verified)

- Co-founded TinySeed with Einar Vollset, announced in an essay dated 2018-10-12
  as "the first startup accelerator designed for bootstrappers"; first batch 2019;
  "backed nearly 200 fast-growing SaaS companies worldwide" as of 2025-01-15
  (robwalling.com/essays/2018/10/12/my-next-act-the-first-startup-accelerator-designed-for-bootstrappers;
  tinyseed.com/latest/venture-returns-with-less-than-venture-risk).
- Also hosts *Startups For the Rest of Us* (840+ episodes), runs MicroConf,
  wrote *The SaaS Playbook* (2023).
- Funds: second "totaling over $25M" (tinyseed.com/latest/second-fund-25m);
  third $10.38M, EMEA, 2023-05-25; fourth $16M, 2025-12-10, 100+ LPs, AUM "more
  than $59M," deploying "over the next three years"
  (tinyseed.com/latest/16m-fourth-saas-fund-raise). Fund 2 LPs named: Bloomberg
  Beta, Eric Ries, Patrick McKenzie, Steli Efti, Rand Fishkin.
- Structure: year-long remote accelerator, two batches a year, "batches of 10-20
  companies," "$120-$300k" per company, lead at that stage, no board seat
  (tinyseed.com/latest/application-preview, updated 2026). Equity of **10–12%** is
  widely reported but only on secondary sites (growthmentor.com, prodevs.io) —
  **UNVERIFIED** against a TinySeed primary page, which renders terms client-side.
- Portfolio markers: Fiscal.ai ($10M Series A, 2025-06-18), Gymdesk ($32.5M, 2024-07-10),
  GlitchSecure, Inbox Zero, Frontend Mentor, StatusGator.
- Claimed performance: "95% of our investments are still operating"; target
  "3-5x," labelled aspirational (tinyseed.com/invest). Vollset, 2025-12-10: "the
  (top decile!) performance of our existing funds."

### 2. Is this deal even in scope? The count

Counted by hand from https://tinyseed.com/portfolio, read 2026-10-04 —
**145 companies listed** (Spring 2022 through Spring 2026 batches).

- **Theatre or performing arts: 0 of 145.** Nothing in the list serves a
  production, a cast, a venue, or a rehearsal process.
- **Arts / education / creative-tools, read generously: 14 of 145 (~10%).**
  Education/training: Sidekick, MOOV, EducateMe, Classcard, Frontend Mentor,
  Rootabl, SessionLab. Creative tools: Anchorpoint, Plainly, Tiny Easy, Press
  Publish, Postpone, Shrpa, INKZUP.
- **Consumer subscriptions under ~$20/month: 0 confirmed of 145.** The only two
  plausible candidates are Frontend Mentor and Postpone, both prosumer; their
  price points could not be verified (pricing pages 404 / client-rendered) —
  **UNVERIFIED**. Every other company on the list sells to a business.
- Caveat on the denominator: Walling said on 2026-03-17 that TinySeed has "210
  portfolio companies," so the public page omits roughly 65 companies from the
  2019–2021 batches. The arts count would have to rise by 5 to change the
  conclusion, which the batch descriptions give no reason to expect.

**Read:** Tablework is outside the demonstrated pattern on two axes at once —
sector (0 theatre, 0 confirmed consumer subs) and price point. The scope
argument has to be that a production is a business and the seat fee is
institutional, not that he has done this before.

### 3. His own stated views, verbatim

On B2C and low ARPU — the central objection (ep. 840, 2026-07-07,
https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-840-5-pm-revisited-starting-over-after-failure-never-shipping-and-more-listener-questions-rob-solo):
> "B2C apps are brutal. They're like eating glass."
> "high churn, high customer support, non-technical users. I mean the list goes on and on and on."
> "once you get into the millions, it's very, very difficult to do that because your churn is so high, so much customer support."

On moats (ep. 836, published 2026-06-09, recorded 2026-05-15,
https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-836-the-5-a-i-moats-acquirers-value-most):
> "Unique features. As developers, we think, 'Oh, I came up with this new innovation and I built this thing that no one else has.' And I was like, 'Nope, false moat.'"
> "Integrations, I actually think with an asterisk. I think integrations in and of themselves are no longer a moat just because it's so much easier to build them."
> "Cost becomes much, much less relevant because switching cost is risk."
Vollset, same episode, on feature uniqueness: > "That's not a thing anymore."
Vollset on AI-native products facing acquirers: > "AI-native SaaS...has higher hurdles."

On distribution (ep. 824, 2026-03-17): of 210 portfolio companies only ~4% rely
on social media; the rest use SEO, cold outreach, paid, events, affiliates,
podcasts, YouTube.
> "Almost no one makes it"
> "Go after something where...you can possibly get in front of some type of demand on an ongoing basis"

On founders (2025-12-10, Fund 4):
> "This fund reflects a deepening commitment to backing founders who are building real businesses, on their own terms, without traditional venture capital."
On what success is made of (essay, 2026-01-17,
https://robwalling.com/essays/the-three-things-you-need-to-build-a-startup-and-why-you-dont-know-which-one-worked):
> "I think about startup success as requiring three things: hard work, luck, and skill."
> "Luck is the variable most people underestimate when they're winning and overestimate when they're losing."

**Seasonality / 6–8-week production cycles: no recorded view found. UNVERIFIED.**
Expect it to arrive as a churn question, where he does have a record.

### 4. The published invitation

https://tinyseed.com/latest/b2b-saas-investing-ambitious-founders — the standing
RFS, read 2026-10-04: "independent, capital-efficient B2B SaaS companies"
aiming at "$5-10M in annual recurring revenue," "built for sustainable growth,
founder control, and a business that supports a life and family," and
> "We're not here to force founders onto a unicorn-or-bust treadmill."
The application preview (updated 2026) is the walkable version: it asks for MRR
now and six months ago, revenue churn, logo churn, ARPC, LTV, marketplace
dependency over 50%, target market size, and the sales funnel. Tablework can
answer every one of those — which is the pitch's best asset and its biggest risk.

### 5. Corrections to widely-repeated claims

- **"TinySeed is a $50M fund."** (starterstory.com headline.) No single fund is
  that size: $25M+, $10.38M, $16M, with AUM "more than $59M" across four
  vehicles. Source: https://tinyseed.com/latest/16m-fourth-saas-fund-raise.
- **"TinySeed does revenue share / is an Earnest-style SEAL."** Its own
  application page: "We don't do revenue- or profit-sharing" — dividends go
  pro-rata on ownership when founders choose to issue them.
  https://tinyseed.com/latest/application-preview.
- **"Earnest Capital and Calm Company Fund are two different funds you could
  pitch."** Same firm, renamed; and it stopped early-stage investing as of
  2024-12-30. https://x.com/tylertringas/status/1873736640226787529.
- **Fund numbering is genuinely inconsistent in TinySeed's own posts**: the
  "third fund" (2023-05-25) is the EMEA fund, while the vehicle marketed as
  "Fund 3" in January 2025 closed as the "fourth fund" in December 2025. Treat
  any ordinal claim with suspicion.
- **"Applications open September 1–9."** True for the 2026 window
  (https://tinyseed.com/latest/application-announcement) but closed now;
  apply.tinyseed.com says the next window is **February 2027** (read 2026-10-04).

### 6. The tests he demonstrably applies

1. "Current MRR? What was MRR six months ago?" — and is there any revenue at
   all: "Applications from companies without revenue will be denied."
   (application preview, 2026)
2. "What is your revenue churn and your logo/customer churn?" (application
   preview) — with the stated prior that low-ARPU products die of it: "your
   churn is so high, so much customer support." (ep. 840, 2026-07-07)
3. "What's your average revenue per customer and your LTV?" (application
   preview) — against his own arithmetic that a ~$20–25 lifetime value leaves
   no acquisition budget at all. (ep. 840)
4. "Is this B2B SaaS?" — "We primarily invest in software services that target
   businesses (B2B SaaS)." (application preview, 2026)
5. "What is your moat, and is it actually one?" — "Nope, false moat." (ep. 836,
   2026-06-09) Features are not a moat; switching cost is.
6. "How do you get in front of demand on an ongoing basis?" — not audience, not
   social: "Almost no one makes it." (ep. 824, 2026-03-17)
7. "How big is your target market, and are you over 50% dependent on a
   marketplace?" (application preview)
8. "Can this be a real business on its own terms — $5–10M ARR, founder-
   controlled — without a unicorn outcome?" (standing RFS, read 2026-10-04)
