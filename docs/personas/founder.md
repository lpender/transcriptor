## The IP gate on THIS product, which is different from impatient.care

**Tablework was created during Gusto employment.** First commit 2026-09-16;
he started at Gusto in June 2026. So it is **not** a prior invention and the
Exhibit B carve-out, which protects prior inventions only, does not cover it.

But the §2870-style test is about whether the invention **relates to the
employer's business**, and a rehearsal-room app for theatre companies plainly
does not relate to payroll, benefits or SMB back office. That is a far cleaner
position than impatient.care's, where the Guideline acquisition put benefits
data squarely inside Gusto's business.

**Still his to confirm, and it is a judgment item, not a research one:** the
actual words of his PIIA, which state's law governs it, and whether anything
was built on employer time or equipment. Nothing in this report should be
acted on commercially until he has read that clause. The loop is not a lawyer
and does not pretend the above is advice.

## What this run must watch for

Both failure modes from the canonical file showed up in the material this run
had to read:

- **Retreating into the build.** The repo has 342 commits, 66 tests and an
  MCP server. None of that is traction, and a deck that leads with it tells
  the counterparty the founder cannot tell cost from evidence.
- **Under-claiming.** His own read of Tablework, unprompted, was *"seems a
  little unwieldy"* (2026-10-04). The record is that he shipped a working
  multi-user product with billing, auth and an AI integration in fifteen days
  of evenings. The honest verdict below is hard on the *market*, and it must
  not be allowed to read as hard on the *builder*.
